Jungfrau region · Switzerland

Valley of 72 waterfalls

A stay-based plan from the village floor to cliff towns and glacier peaks.

Base in Lauterbrunnen. Pick a day card for the full rhythm — morning, afternoon, dinner, and how to move.

Weather rule: If the summits are clear, do Schilthorn or Kleine Scheidegg first. Save indoor Trümmelbach Falls for a cloudy or rainy stretch.

Day plans

Day one

Valley of waterfalls

Settle into the village and let the cliffs do the work. Short walks, big views, early dinner.

Easy Village ~4 hours out
Morning

Arrive & drop bags

Train to Lauterbrunnen (via Interlaken Ost). Hotel or apartment is usually a short walk from the station. Grab a map at the tourist office on the main street.

Late morning

Staubbach Falls

  • Walk south through the village to the base of Staubbach — the 297 m ribbon that makes the valley famous.
  • If the path is open (typically late spring–autumn), climb the short tunnel trail behind the spray for the lookout.
  • Church of St. Mary and the cemetery above town: classic valley photo with the falls behind the spire.
Afternoon

Valley floor wander

Follow the river path toward the campsite and Weisse Lütschine. Optional short hop on bus 141 if legs are tired. Coffee and a pastry in the village (Airtime Café is a reliable stop).

Evening

Dinner in the valley

Hotel restaurant or Horner Pub for a casual plate. Watch the cliffs pick up last light — waterfalls glow after rain.

Pack a light rain shell even on a blue-sky day. Spray from Staubbach and sudden valley showers are normal.
Day two

Inside the mountain

Europe’s only glacier waterfalls you walk inside — then the quiet south end of the valley.

Indoor falls Wet stairs Good in rain
Morning

Trümmelbach Falls

  • Bus 141 from Lauterbrunnen station (~15 min) or walk the valley path (~45–60 min).
  • Ten falls drain the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau glaciers — lift + tunnels + metal walkways.
  • Allow about an hour inside. Closed in winter; typically open April–November.
Lunch

Falls café or picnic

Eat at the Trümmelbach restaurant, or continue south with snacks. Waterproof jacket and grippy shoes — it is loud, dark, and slippery.

Afternoon

Stechelberg & Gimmelwald

Bus or walk to Stechelberg Schilthornbahn. Cable car up one stop to Gimmelwald — tiny car-free hamlet, benches, and cliff-edge views. Wander, then ride back down (or stay up if you want to preview Mürren).

Evening

Return to Lauterbrunnen

Bus 141 from Stechelberg. Slow dinner; this day is wetter and louder than it looks on a map.

Skip combining this with Schilthorn. The falls deserve unhurried time, and summit weather is better as its own morning.
Day three

Mürren & Schilthorn

The west wall of the valley: car-free Mürren, then Piz Gloria if the peaks are out.

Clear-sky day Cable cars Full day
Go up early. Clouds often build after midday. Buy Schilthorn tickets the evening before only if the forecast is solid — no refund for fog.
Morning — option A (clear)

Schilthorn first

Bus to Stechelberg → cable car via Gimmelwald and Mürren to Schilthorn (2,970 m). 360° Eiger–Mönch–Jungfrau views, Bond exhibit, rotating restaurant. Do not linger if queues are long — the view is the prize.

Morning — option B (cloudy)

Skip the summit

Cable car Lauterbrunnen → Grütschalp, then the BLM train along the cliff to Mürren. Same village, none of the whiteout ticket cost.

Afternoon

Mürren village

  • Stroll the main lane: chalets, Allmendhubel funicular (optional flower/alpine garden views).
  • Lunch on a terrace facing the Jungfrau massif.
  • Easy cliff path toward Grütschalp (~1 hour) or ride the train + cable car down.
Evening

Loop home

Grütschalp cable car drops you almost on top of Lauterbrunnen station. Fondue or rösti back in the valley.

A Jungfrau Travel Pass or Swiss Travel Pass covers most valley lifts; Schilthorn is often a discount, not free. Check your pass before buying.
Day four

Wengen panorama

The sunny terrace across the valley — gentler light, wide meadows, and a classic ridge walk.

Car-free Ridge walk Moderate
Morning

Train to Wengen

Cogwheel from Lauterbrunnen (about 15 min). No cars in the village. Coffee with Jungfrau staring straight at you, then wander the main street and church viewpoint.

Midday

Männlichen

Cable car from Wengen up to Männlichen. The Royal Walk / Panorama Trail toward Kleine Scheidegg is the highlight: wide, well-marked, huge north-face views without technical hiking. ~1.5 hours at an easy pace.

Afternoon

Down via Wengen or Grindelwald

Turn around to Wengen, or continue to Kleine Scheidegg and ride down the other side toward Grindelwald for a longer loop (train back via Interlaken or Zweilütschinen). Keep it simple if you want an early evening in the valley.

Evening

Sunset from Wengen or home

Wengen catches later sun than the valley floor. Descend before the last train if you are not staying up there.

This walk is exposed. Windbreaker + sun protection. In winter the same ridge is ski terrain — check seasonal closures.
Day five

Eiger & Kleine Scheidegg

Stand under the north face. Treat Jungfraujoch as an optional extra, not the whole day.

High alpine Optional splurge Start early
Morning

Train to Kleine Scheidegg

Lauterbrunnen → Wengen → Kleine Scheidegg. Sit on the left going up for Eiger close-ups. Walk around the pass: restaurants, trailheads, and the wall filling the sky.

Optional add-on

Jungfraujoch — Top of Europe

  • Another cogwheel into the mountain to 3,454 m: glacier plateau, Sphinx terrace, ice palace.
  • Expensive and crowded. Worth it on a crystal day; skip it in cloud — you pay for a view you will not get.
  • Reserve a timed ticket in high season. Half-fare / Jungfrau Pass discounts matter here.
Afternoon without Jungfraujoch

Eiger Trail (if snow-free)

Kleine Scheidegg toward Alpiglen along the base of the north face — one of the best “I am actually here” walks in the region. Otherwise linger at the pass and ride down via Grindelwald for a different descent.

Evening

Back to the valley

Last trains are earlier than city hours. Celebrate with something slow — you have covered both walls of the valley.

Altitude + sun is a real combo. Water, layers, and sunglasses even if it looks mild in Lauterbrunnen (800 m vs 2,000–3,400 m up top).
